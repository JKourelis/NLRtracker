# NLR signature set — what each signature is, how they overlap, what they mean

The authoritative data is in three generated tables. This document explains them.

| File | Rows |
|---|---:|
| [`../module/nlr_signatures.tsv`](../module/nlr_signatures.tsv) | 231 signatures |
| [`../module/nlr_classes.tsv`](../module/nlr_classes.tsv) | 41 domain classes |
| [`../module/nlr_subclass_rules.tsv`](../module/nlr_subclass_rules.tsv) | 1 multi-signature rule |

All three are regenerated by [`../module/build_signature_table.py`](../module/build_signature_table.py)
from the InterProScan datadir's `entries.json`, which is the authoritative offline signature map for
a given InterPro release. Current release: **InterPro 109.0**.

Evidence base for every curated claim below:

- **RefPlantNLR** — 481 experimentally validated plant NLRs with published subclass assignments
  (PLOS Biology `pbio.3001124.s013`, Table S1). 481 of 481 identifiers matched exactly.
- **A full *Nicotiana tabacum* proteome** — 75 127 proteins, used wherever RefPlantNLR was too
  narrow to decide.

Full analysis, with the contingency tables:
[`signatures.md`](signatures.md).

## 1. The two things a signature can do

Every signature has exactly one **region** role and, separately, an optional **subclass** role.
Conflating them is the single easiest way to get this wrong.

- A **region** role says *"this part of the protein is a TIR / an NB-ARC / an LRR"*. It determines
  coordinates: what gets drawn, and what gets written to the per-region FASTA.
- A **subclass** role says *"this protein belongs to the CC-NLR / TIR-NLR lineage"*. It determines
  classification only, and never coordinates.

### Why this matters: the winged-helix Pfams

`PF23559` (WHD_DRP) and `PF23282` (WHD_ROQ1) are the strongest lineage discriminators in the whole
set:

| Signature | CC-NLR | TIR-NLR | CCG10 | RPW8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PF23559` | **343/347** | 0/105 | 8/19 | 0/10 |
| `PF23282` | 0/347 | **99/105** | 0/19 | 0/10 |

But both are **NB-ARC winged-helix domains**. They sit *inside* the NB-ARC, not at the N-terminus.

The decisive evidence: of the 19 CCG10-NLRs, **8 carry `PF23559`, and none of those 8 carries any
true CC signature** (`PF18052`, `cd14798`, `G3DSA:1.20.5.4130`).

So they are assigned region `NB-ARC` and subclass evidence `CC-NLR` / `TIR-NLR`. Treating
`PF23559` as a CC *region* would draw a CC block on top of the NB-ARC in every CC-NLR figure and
corrupt every extracted CC sequence. Used correctly it lifts CC-lineage coverage from 85%
(`PF18052`, the previous best) to **99%**.

The same split applies to the two generic winged-helix signatures inherited from 1.x, which turn
out not to be interchangeable: `G3DSA:1.10.10.10` is 333/347 CC-NLR, `SSF46785` is 54/105 TIR-NLR.

### Boundary arbitration

Raw signature coordinates overlap. Arbitration runs in this order, with NB-ARC (or a bare P-loop)
as the anchor:

1. Build the NB-ARC region by merging P-loop, HD1, winged-helix and NB-ARC motif evidence.
2. Truncate each N-terminal region — TIR, CC, RPW8, R1 — to end before the NB-ARC starts.
3. Truncate the LRR to start after the NB-ARC ends.
4. Truncate the C-JID to start after the LRR ends.
5. Subtract every canonical region from the integrated-domain regions, splitting them if needed.

Regions trimmed out of existence are dropped **and** reported in `raw/logs/dropped_regions.tsv`,
grouped by the rule that removed them. Disjointness is asserted on every run.

### Merge groups

Classes sharing a `merge_group` collapse into one interval when they overlap, and the highest
`precedence` names the result.

| Group | Members, highest precedence first | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `nbarc` | `NBARC` 40 › `NACHT` 30 › `PLOOP` 20 › `nbarc-motif` / `Winged-helix` / `nacht-winged-helix` 10 | A P-loop overlapping real NB-ARC evidence becomes NB-ARC. The winged-helix and motif classes exist only to extend the interval and are removed afterwards (`drop_after`). |
| `rpw8` | `RPW8` 40 › `MLKL` 30 › `CblN` 20 | Preserves the 1.x precedence where RPW8 wins over an overlapping MLKL or CblN. |
| `id` | specific domains 40 › `OTHER` / `PLOOP-OTHER` 20 › super-structure repeats (`ANK`, `TPR`, `WD40`, `ARM`, `a/b`) 10 | Each specific domain is merged against `OTHER` **pairwise**, so two unrelated integrated domains can never be joined through a shared `OTHER` annotation. |

### Notes on individual entries

**`PF23559` — the best CC marker in the set.** 98% purity, 99% coverage, beating `PF18052` at 85%.
It also hits 8/19 CCG10-NLRs, which is biologically consistent since CCG10 is a CC-lineage clade,
and is the reason lineage-only evidence cannot separate CC-NLR from CCG10-NLR.

**`PF23282` — diagnostic TIR-NLR**, 100% purity, 94% coverage. It adds nothing to TIR *detection*
(three existing signatures already cover 105/105) but it extends the NB-ARC over the WHD and is a
clean lineage marker.

**The four LRR Pfams are NLR-specific**, unlike the generic `PF00560`, `SSF52058` and `SSF52047`.
That makes them valuable for extraction: they raise LRR-region confidence without pulling in every
plant LRR protein. All four are InterPro type `repeat`, so they emit one row per repeat and are
suppressed from the published GFF3 as noise while still contributing `REPEAT` rows to
`Domains.tsv`.

**`PF23598` has no subclass role.** 87% purity is too low: it hits 259/347 CC-NLRs but also 25
TIR-NLRs and 3 RPW8-NLRs. It is region and extraction evidence only.

**`PF25895` fires 6 times in 75 127 proteins and 0 times in RefPlantNLR.** It was nearly omitted on
the RefPlantNLR evidence alone — a reminder that 481 curated proteins are a narrow corpus. Its
InterPro annotation describes exactly the intended role: a WHD "typically found C-terminal and
adjacent to the NB-ARC domain IPR002182 […] characteristic of nucleotide-binding leucine-rich
repeat (NLR) immune receptors in plants".

**`SSF47668` belongs to MLKL, not CblN.** It shares `IPR036537` with `G3DSA:1.20.930.20`, which 1.x
labelled `(CblN)`. The entry is the **N-terminal four-helical bundle superfamily**, and that bundle
"can also be found in the N terminus of mammalian MLKL". This is consistent with the known
structural similarity between plant RPW8/CCR domains and the MLKL 4H bundle, and it explains why
`(CblN)` already merged with `(RPW8)` and `(MLKL)`. `SSF47668` is the mammalian-biased member and
returns 0 across 75 127 plant proteins; the plant route runs through `cd21037` (38) and
`G3DSA:1.20.930.20` (36).

**`PF06760` is the weak link in MLKL detection.** InterPro classifies it as `DUF1221` / IPR010632,
*"Protein of unknown function"* — not MLKL. It fires 14 times against `cd21037`'s 38, so `cd21037`
leads the group.

## 4. CCG10-NLR

Before 2.0.0 this subclass could not be assigned. The subclass column could only ever produce the
ambiguous string `"CC-NLR or CCR-NLR or CCG10-NLR"`, and there was no CCG10 signature in the
codebase at all. `G3DSA:1.20.5.4220`, cited for CCG10 in earlier notes, **does not exist in
InterPro 108.0 or 109.0**.

The obstacle is biological: **no CC domain is detectable in any CCG10-NLR.** All 19 in RefPlantNLR
have a `Structure` of `NL` or `ONL` in Table S1 — no `C` — and 0/19 hit `PF18052`, `cd14798` or
`G3DSA:1.20.5.4130`.

`PF23247` (LRR_RPS2, named after RPS2, itself a G10-clade member) alone is a coin flip. Gating it
on the absence of a real CC domain makes it exact:

| Rule | Selects | CCG10 found | False positives | Precision |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| `PF23247` alone | 21 | 11/19 | 10 | 52% |
| **`PF23247` AND NOT (`PF18052` \| `cd14798`)** | **11** | **11/19** | **0** | **100%** |

That is the rule in `nlr_subclass_rules.tsv`. It is applied **after** the diagnostic TIR and RPW8
routes, so a clear N-terminal domain always wins, and **before** the generic CC routes, because
CCG10-NLRs have no CC domain to find.

Because it recovers 11 of 19, a positive call is trustworthy and a negative proves nothing. The
result is therefore reported as **`CCG10-NLR (probable)`**, which keeps that asymmetry visible.
On RefPlantNLR it is 11/11 exact.

An unbiased enrichment scan over all 231 signatures found no other CCG10 marker. The only
CCG10-enriched hits are Ulp1 protease and ubiquitin-related domains (`SSF54001`, `PS50600`,
`PF02902`, `G3DSA:3.30.310.130`, `G3DSA:1.10.418.20`, 4/19 each) — the integrated domains of the
four `Rpp1-R*` entries, so ID annotations rather than clade markers.

## 5. Audit of the inherited signature set

Every accession in the 1.x `case_when` chain was checked against InterPro 109.0.

| | |
|---|---:|
| distinct signature accessions | 222 |
| MEME/FIMO motifs | 12 |
| resolved with an InterPro entry | 218 |
| **withdrawn from InterPro** | **0** |

Nothing in a signature set frozen in 2021 has been withdrawn. The one accession that fails lookup
is `CJID`, which is not an InterPro accession — it is the internal label for HMMER hits from the
Adachi HMM.

**Name resolution simplified.** With InterProScan 6 TSV input, the signature description arrives in
column 6 and the InterPro entry name in column 13. Only nine accessions have neither and still need
a name supplied locally:

`G3DSA:1.20.5.4130`, `G3DSA:1.10.533.20`, `G3DSA:3.30.70.100`, `G3DSA:3.10.20.370`,
`G3DSA:1.10.340.70`, `cd00200`, `cd02947`, `cd02989`, `PR00364`

That replaces most of the hardcoded fallback block in 1.x. The generator flags them as
`needs_name_fallback = yes`.

**Drift found.** `G3DSA:1.20.5.4130` is **unintegrated** in InterPro ≥ 108.0 — no entry, no
description — so its local name is load-bearing and must stay. `cd14798` now integrates into
`IPR038005` ("Virus X resistance protein-like, coiled-coil domain") rather than `IPR041118`.

**Dead code found.** `PF18461` and `G3DSA:1.10.533.20` each appeared twice in the `case_when`
chain, both times mapping to `(CARD)`. The second branch could never fire. The generator reports
duplicate assignments and keeps the first.

## 6. Classification and how much to trust it

Scored against the 481 published RefPlantNLR subclasses. `GR`/RNL maps to **CCR-NLR** in
NLRtracker's vocabulary, which reserves the bare name `RPW8` for NLR-associated RPW8 proteins that
lack an NB-ARC.

| | n | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| exact | **453** / 481 | **94.2%** |
| ambiguous call containing the truth | 23 | 4.8% |
| **wrong** | **1** | **0.2%** |

The `subclass_support` column records the evidence tier, and it predicts reliability:

| Tier | n | exact | ambiguous | **wrong** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `domain` | 443 | 439 | 0 | **0** |
| `lineage` | 23 | 3 | 20 | **0** |
| `probable` | 11 | 11 | 0 | **0** |
| `motif` | 2 | 0 | 2 | **0** |
| `supporting` | 1 | 0 | 1 | **0** |
| none | 1 | 0 | 0 | **1** |

Zero wrong calls in every tier that carries evidence. The single wrong call, `SpNBS-LRR`, has no
subclass evidence of any kind and is reported as `UNDETERMINED`.

### An explicit ambiguity beats a confident guess

The first implementation reported a bare `CC-NLR` whenever `PF23559` was present without a
detectable CC domain. **7 of those 23 calls were actually CCG10-NLRs** — a 30% error rate, for the
reason in section 1. Those calls now report `CC-NLR or CCG10-NLR`, which moved 20 calls from wrong
to honestly uncertain and took the wrong count from 18 to 1.

## 7. Known gaps

- **CCG10-NLR coverage is 11/19.** The remaining 8 carry `PF23559` but not `PF23247`, so they land
  in `CC-NLR or CCG10-NLR`. Closing this needs a signature that does not yet exist.
- **MLKL cannot be validated on RefPlantNLR** — it contains NLRs, not MLKLs. `cd21037` fires 38
  times in a real proteome, which is the only evidence available.
- **`PF25895` rests on 6 observations.** Worth re-checking on more proteomes.
- **CblN evidence is nearly absent** from RefPlantNLR (1 hit), so the CCR-NLR route via CblN is
  unexercised there.
